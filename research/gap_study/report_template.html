Gap and Go Study

Do stocks that open with a big gap keep moving that way through the day and the following weeks? Tested on S&P 500 stocks, .

Short answer

  1. On average, no. A gap of any size, up or down, is followed by roughly the same return as the market from the open: the same day, the next day, and 5 or 20 days later. Gap size and later returns are essentially uncorrelated (rank correlation ).
  2. Volume is the one split that matters. Gaps on 3× normal volume kept going the same day: for up gaps and for down gaps, vs the market. Low-volume down gaps tended to bounce instead.
  3. But that edge stays inside the gap day. Daily data only tells you the day's volume after the close, and from the close onward those same stocks did nothing special. Whether you can spot the heavy-volume gaps early enough, from premarket or first-15-minute volume, is exactly what Phase B tests.

Gaps do not usually "fill": an up gap of 8% or more traded back to the prior close the same day only of the time.

How it was measured

Universe
current S&P 500 members, price ≥ $5, average daily dollar volume ≥ $5M, stock-days.
Gap
Open vs the previous close. With daily bars this is the overnight and premarket move combined.
Entry
At the open of the gap day, and separately at its close (once the day's volume is known).
Returns
Stock minus SPY over the same window, then minus the stock's own average for that horizon. The second step removes the drift that comes from only including companies that are in the index today.
Volume
The gap day's volume divided by its 20-day average.
Earnings
Gap on the first session after a reported earnings date (Yahoo earnings calendar).
Significance
t-statistics use standard errors clustered by date, because gaps bunch up on macro news days. Treat |t| below 2 as noise. Results in grey are not significant.

Other splits (gaps of 4% or more)

Earnings gaps, the market regime and whether the whole market gapped don't change the picture. The two halves of the decade even point in opposite directions, which is what noise looks like.

What this can't tell you

  • Survivorship. Only today's index members are included. The own-average adjustment removes most of the effect, but companies that collapsed and left the index are missing entirely.
  • Large caps only. The classic gap-and-go setup is small, low-float stocks with a news catalyst. These results say nothing about those, and free data for them is much worse.
  • Premarket is blended in. A daily open can't separate after-hours, premarket and the opening auction, or tell you premarket volume.
  • Costs. Figures are before costs. A round trip at the open in these stocks costs roughly in spread and slippage, more on the most volatile gap days.
  • Earnings timing. Yahoo's calendar sometimes lacks the report time; those dates were matched to both possible sessions.

Phase B: test it with real premarket data

  • About 2 years of 1-minute bars including premarket (Polygon's free tier), for the same stocks.
  • Measure premarket volume and the first 5 and 15 minutes of regular-session volume, which are known early enough to act on.
  • Test whether those early volume readings pick out the heavy-volume gaps that kept moving, and how much of the move is left after the first 15 minutes.

This is research, not a trading recommendation. A positive Phase B result would be a hypothesis to paper-trade first.
